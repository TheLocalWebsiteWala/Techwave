Good tools rarely announce themselves. They sit quietly in a bag or on a desk until the moment they're needed, and then they simply work. That is the standard we hold every product to, and it is the lens we use when writing about the wider category.

Start with what you actually use

Before adding anything new, spend a week noticing what you reach for. The pattern is usually clearer than expected: a charger, a pair of headphones, one bag. Everything else is optional, and optional things are where clutter begins.

  • Choose one charging standard and commit to it everywhere.
  • Prefer objects that fold, stack or nest into what you already carry.
  • Replace two single-purpose items with one that does both well.

Buy for the long middle

Most gear is bought for an unusual day — a long flight, a big shoot — and then used on ordinary ones. Optimise for the long middle instead: the commute, the coffee shop, the desk. That's where a product earns its keep.

The result is a smaller kit that feels bigger, because everything in it is something you genuinely wanted to bring.
